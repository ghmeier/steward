# Phase 10 research: what offline catch-up builds on

## Question

What in the engine, saves, desk, decrees and runner does offline catch-up build on, which parts can take "rates in force times compressed time" in one step, and where should catch-up hook in so it runs before the first frame and before input?

## Findings

### 1. Time and how production is computed

**Game time.** `stepMs` is 100 (`data/tuning.json`), so the game runs 10 ticks per second. A season is `ticksPerSeason` 600 ticks (one minute), and a year of four seasons is 2,400 ticks (four minutes). A real week is 6,048,000 ticks, or about 2,520 game years. Seasons come from the tick alone: `seasonAt(tick)` at `src/core/economy.ts:188-192`, and `seasonEnds` is `tick % ticksPerSeason === 0` (`economy.ts:184-186`).

**Clock and session.** `advance` (`src/clock.ts:31-52`) turns elapsed milliseconds into whole steps, caps them at `maxStepsPerFrame` (50), and adds the excess to `droppedMs` (`clock.ts:44-49`). `createClock` returns 0 on its first frame (`clock.ts:70-73`). `Session` (`src/tui/session.ts:33-37`) builds the clock from `game.tuning.stepMs`, and `frame()` (`session.ts:50-79`) calls `game.steps.step(queued, ...)` once per step, handing all queued commands to the first step of the frame. The app calls `frame()` every 50 ms (`src/tui/app.ts:20`, `app.ts:316-319`) and autosaves every 30 s (`app.ts:21`, `app.ts:320-323`). Nothing reads `droppedMs` today.

**Step order** (`src/core/step.ts:39-66`): the tick is incremented, then commands are applied in order, then `runFlows` (economy, then decrees; `step.ts:24-28`), then desk, governors, crown, illusion, promotion and unlocks.

**A rate per tick already exists.** `ProjectionManager.flows()` (`src/core/projection.ts:17-46`) clones the game, advances the clone's tick by one, runs exactly `runFlows`, and returns the per-resource difference as `rates`, plus `growthPerTick` and `eatenPerTick`. The ledger shows these as rates (`src/core/view.ts:151-165`). It leaves out commands and the desk on purpose. This is the natural "rates in force at exit": evaluate it once and multiply by N.

**Production itself is procedural per tick.** `EconomyManager.run` (`economy.ts:304-341`) counts working peasants and governors' villages (`src/core/delegates.ts:106-124`), adds wood and ore through `add`, then eats grain before storing the harvest; a shortfall advances the famine instead (`economy.ts:288-302`). Decrees then sell or feed what the tick produced, or everything above a threshold for an "above N" rule (`src/core/decree-engine.ts:134-192`). Feeding grows `economy.growth` and admits newcomers per `grainPerPeasant` (`economy.ts:375-391`).

**A closed form** for the economy is: `rates = game.projection.flows().rates`, then for each resource `add(resources, r, Math.max(rates[r] * N, -current), context)` with a context at the current tick. `add` (`src/core/math.ts:44-75`) already applies the store cap, keeps stock that is already above a cap, and clamps at `maxResourceValue`, but it does not floor at zero, so the `Math.max` is needed. One multiplication per resource is deterministic.

**What a one-step application must handle**, because the per-tick system is not linear:

- **Caps.** Base caps are 500 grain, 200 wood and 100 ore (`data/tuning.json`), which production fills within minutes. Any absence past the grace window will usually leave capped stores at their cap, so the visible offline gain is mostly coin from sell decrees. A projected rate taken at a full store is already zero for that store, because `add` returned the overflow as lost.
- **Seasons.** The projection reads the yield of the next tick's season, and winter's yield is 0 (`tuning.json` `seasonYield`). Leaving in winter would project a negative grain rate and no harvest for the whole absence. For any absence longer than a year (four minutes) an average over the four seasons (0.875 at current tuning) is the honest rate; see open question 2.
- **Starvation.** A negative grain rate must clamp grain at zero and must not run `advanceFamine`, since nothing dies while away.
- **Births.** A "feed" harvest decree moves grain into `growth` and admits newcomers. The grain rate already subtracts the fed grain, so if births are suppressed the player pays grain for nothing; see open question 3.
- **Prospecting.** The extraction doctrine pays coin only on the season-end tick (`economy.ts:348-355`), so a one-tick projection shows a coin rate of 0 or a whole season's payout. It needs its own term: ore rate times `prospectingShare` times N.
- **Labor settling and grudges.** Settling moves one peasant per tick (`economy.ts:472-498`), and grudged peasants count as idle (`delegates.ts:114`). If the tick does not advance (see below), both stay as they were at exit, which matches "nothing happens".
- **Governor hoards** accrue in `governors.run` (`src/core/governors.ts:435-444`), not in `runFlows`, so the projection leaves them out. That is consistent with "no governor activity while away".

**Recommendation on the tick.** Catch-up should not advance `state.tick`. Every deadline in state is an absolute tick: crown `dueAt` and `nextDemandTick` (`src/core/crown.ts:431-440`), audit `auditAt` and `dueAt`, `debtSince`, `lastAuditClosedAt`, governors' `termEndsAt`, everyone's `grudgeUntil`, and the desk's `nextArrivalTick`. Leaving the tick alone freezes all of them, which is exactly "no desk events, no crown deadlines, no governor turnover". It also keeps the season at return equal to the season at exit.

### 2. Illusion: fading and debt growth

There is no "illusion decay" separate from fading: conjured coin and grain fade each tick by `fadePerTick` 0.00115 (`src/core/illusion.ts:567-572`, `data/illusion.json`), a half-life of about one season. Fading does not create debt; only spending conjured units does (`illusion.ts:296-303`).

Debt grows only by interest: on each season-end tick, `chargeOnPrincipal(interestPerSeason)` adds `principal * rate` to each resource's debt (`illusion.ts:559-575`). Interest is on principal, so it never compounds. The rate is `interestPerSeason` 0.05 times the unlock modifier `"interest"` (`illusion.ts:508-512`).

Both compose cleanly over N ticks:

- Fading is geometric: `conjured * powInt(1 - fade, N)`. `powInt` (`math.ts:4-19`) is the deterministic power the core must use; `Math.pow` and `Math.log` are banned in `src/core` (`src/core/core-boundary.test.ts:37-48`). At N of six million it takes about 23 squarings and underflows to zero.
- Interest is linear in time: `debt += principal * rate * (realTicks / ticksPerSeason)`. A fractional season is fine because nothing rounds debt.

**Scale warning.** At the current rate a real week is 10,080 seasons, which adds 504 times the principal. The "separate offline rate" therefore cannot be "interest per season". It should be a rate per real hour or day, and it should still be multiplied by the `"interest"` modifier so the Archive upgrade that lowers interest keeps working offline.

**Audits on return.** `runAudit` (`illusion.ts:535-556`) announces auditors when debt value reaches `debtShare * ceiling` (adjusted by crown favor and prestige escalation) or when debt is `ageTicks` old (`illusion.ts:522-533`). If the tick does not advance, age never triggers from an absence, but a debt jump will trip the share test on the first played tick. The auditors are then `warningTicks` (600, so 60 s) away, and the player has `deadlineTicks` (900, so 90 s) to answer. "Back" requires real stock covering the whole debt (`illusion.ts:436-443`, `desk.ts:338-353`); "deepen" costs `deepenCost` 0.2 times the debt's coin value. Failing seizes stock and dismisses a governor (`illusion.ts:471-491`). An audit already on the road or on the desk at exit keeps its absolute deadline, so it lands soon after return. A large debt jump will very likely end in a failed audit within about two and a half minutes of launch; see open question 4.

### 3. Desk, a return event and governors' reports

**Definitions.** `data/desk.json` holds `slots` (3), `arrival` (900 to 1,800 ticks), `grudgeTicks`, `scale`, and per-kind `weight`, `unlock` and `templates`. Schemas are in `src/core/desk-tuning.ts:78-211`. A template has `text`, at least two `options`, an `expiry` option id, and `expired` text. The expiry option must not gain resources, grudge or add a peasant (`desk-tuning.ts:90-119`). Placeholders are checked per kind by `textWithPlaceholders` (`desk-tuning.ts:25-29`).

**Creation.** Drawn kinds are listed in `DRAWN_KINDS` (`src/core/desk.ts:26-33`) and picked by weight from the `disputes` RNG stream in `arrive` (`desk.ts:524-568`). Audits are not drawn: `placeAudit` (`desk.ts:574-589`) picks the template by `nextDisputeId % templates.length`, so it consumes no randomness. A return event should be placed the same way, keeping it out of `DRAWN_KINDS` so the golden hash and every replay stay unchanged.

**Slots and expiry.** Entries are stored oldest first. Only audits have a deadline. Other entries leave when settled, or when the desk holds more than `slots` and `run` expires the oldest non-audit entry, applying its expiry option's cost (`desk.ts:633-640`). Placing a return event on a full desk would expire the oldest existing entry on the first step and charge its expiry cost; see open question 5.

**Resolution.** `settle` and `settleOldest` (`desk.ts:410-460`) check `shortfall` against apparent stock, then apply the option's deltas through `illusion.spend` for losses. `deltasOf` (`desk.ts:302-314`) multiplies template amounts by the entry's `scale` and rounds. That `scale` field (`desk.ts:46-49`) is the obvious place to store "time away times how extreme the policies were", so the costs shown are the costs applied.

**What a new kind needs.** It needs a name in `DESK_KINDS` (but not `DRAWN_KINDS`), a variant in the `DeskEntry` union and `deskEntry` schema (`desk.ts:72-129`), a kind in `deskTuningSchema.kinds` with its own placeholder list (and probably no `weight`, like `auditKind` at `desk-tuning.ts:179-190`), templates in `data/desk.json`, a branch in `fill` for its placeholders (`desk.ts:282-299`), and copy. The `settle` command's `kind` enum follows `DESK_KINDS` automatically (`src/core/commands.ts:29-33`). The content should be data (templates with base amounts) and the numbers generated (the `scale` and any figures in the text stored on the entry), because the save must show the same text after reload.

**Governors' unreliable reports.** A governor's reported output is real output times the product of their traits' `report` factors (`governors.ts:204-237`): braggart 1.4, skimmer 1.25, empire-builder 1.2 (`data/governors.json`). The ledger adds `reportGap` to its rates unless the `precision.reports` unlock (`TRUE_REPORTS`) is owned (`view.ts:153-165`, `governors.ts:291-302`), and the governor panel shows `truth` only with that unlock (`governors.ts:245-277`). A "while you were away" report could quote the offline gain plus the same gap multiplied by the time away, attributed to the governor with the largest exaggeration (the same ordering `dismissAfterFailedAudit` uses at `governors.ts:418-432`). With no governor serving, it would need a non-governor voice, such as a clerk.

### 4. Policies in force

There are no rations or taxes. The policy outputs are:

- harvest (grain): `feed`, `sell` or `stockpile` (`src/core/decree.ts:312`);
- wood and ore: `sell` or `stockpile` (`decree.ts:313`);
- labor: a named allocation from `laborAllocations` (`balanced`, `farming`, `farm`, `forest`, `mine`) or a custom split such as "farm 60 forest 30 mine 10" (`src/core/labor-split.ts`).

Decrees choose a policy per tick from conditions over stock, population, season, debt, crown demands and other decrees' choices (`decree.ts:18-52`, `src/core/conditions.ts:7-35`). Only the labor choice is saved (`posting.labor.policy`, `src/core/labor.ts:10-35`). The other choices come back from `DecreeEngine.run` as `chosen` (`decree-engine.ts:143-174`), but `runFlows` discards that value (`step.ts:24-28`). Returning `chosen` from `runFlows` would let the projection, which already runs it on a clone, report the policies in force at exit at no extra cost.

Candidate measures of "extreme": a labor share of 1 on one job (the `farm`, `forest` and `mine` presets), especially when no one farms; selling the whole harvest; a negative projected grain rate (the village was eating its reserve at exit); and the illusion's exposure (`books` "strained" or "rotten", `illusion.ts:277-284`). The user should pick which of these count; see open question 6.

### 5. Saves and where catch-up hooks in

**Schema.** `SCHEMA_VERSION` is 20 (`src/core/state.ts:12`). The save file is `{ schemaVersion, savedAt, contentHash, state }` (`src/save/save.ts:36-41`). `savedAt` is outside `state`, defaults to `new Date().toISOString()` at write time (`save.ts:299`), and is only checked to be a string (`save.ts:234-236`). Upgrades are a chain of in-place rewrites keyed by version (`save.ts:69-225`). Adding a desk kind is a new union variant, so old saves stay valid without an upgrade; a bump is only needed if catch-up adds fields to state.

**Load.** `main.ts` calls `prepareStartup(saveDir, tuning, newSeed)` (`src/tui/main.ts:12`), which loads, wraps the state with `Game.fromState`, runs `repealStale`, and returns a notice (`app.ts:27-65`). `startApp` then builds the `Session` and draws (`app.ts:87-101`, `app.ts:313-314`) before starting the frame timer. So `prepareStartup` is the place: after `repealStale`, before any `Session` exists, and before any key handler is attached. It is also where a notice for the return already has a slot. `repealStale` is the precedent for a load-time state change outside `step`.

**Wall clock.** The core may not read `Date` (`core-boundary.test.ts:37-38`). `prepareStartup` has no wall-clock parameter, and the `now` passed to `startApp` is `performance.now()`, which is monotonic and unrelated to `savedAt` (`main.ts:35`). Catch-up needs a new injected `wallNow: () => number` (`Date.now` in `main.ts`). It should parse `savedAt` with `Date.parse`, treat NaN as zero (tests write `savedAt: "x"` and `""`, `src/save/save.test.ts:71`, `src/tui/app.test.ts:157`), and clamp negatives to zero. The compressed tick count must be an integer computed deterministically; doing the logarithm outside `src/core` or with an integer log2 inside it both work.

**Command or not.** The session's `commandLog` lives only in memory and is never saved (`session.ts:18`, `session.ts:61`). Replays exist only in the runner, from a saved state plus a command log file. Making catch-up a `step` command would have three costs. It would cost a tick. It would run after the first frame, because `createClock`'s first frame returns 0, so the first view would show the pre-return state. It would apply flows and desk arrivals in the same tick. `endPosting` is a player decision made during play, so it fits a command; catch-up is a load-time transform like `repealStale`. I recommend a core method that takes integer real and compressed tick counts and needs no randomness, called from `prepareStartup`, with the runner able to call the same method so it can be replayed.

### 6. Runner, tests and determinism

The runner loads with `readSaveFile` and `Game.fromState` and then runs `repealStale` (`src/runner/sim.ts:158-169`). It never looks at `savedAt`. If catch-up stays out of `parseSave` and `loadGame`, the split-run determinism test (`src/core/determinism.test.ts:72-105`) and the round-trip test (`determinism.test.ts:108-133`, which already normalizes `savedAt`) are unaffected. If catch-up went into `parseSave`, the split-run test would break, because resuming would apply real wall time. `test/golden.json` hashes state after 10,000 steps with no load, so it changes only if the return kind joins the drawn kinds or tuning changes the simulation.

To simulate "left for a week":

- **Unit level:** build a game with `sampleVillage` (`test/helpers.ts:345-355`) plus conjuring and forging unlocks, run some steps, create debt, then call the catch-up method with real ticks of 6,048,000. Compare against a clone given a short absence. The test should assert three things: gains below `rate × realTicks`, debt greater than or equal to before, and a return entry on the desk. `servedVillage` (`helpers.ts:384-391`) is for ending a posting and is not a good fit.
- **Startup level:** `app.test.ts:129-200` already drives `prepareStartup` with temporary save directories. With an injected `wallNow` it can write a save with a fixed `savedAt` and load it a week later.
- **Runner:** an `--away <seconds>` flag on `sim.ts` (valid only with `--load`) would let a simulation measure return costs and let a strategy test cover them.
- **Manual TUI check:** `STEWARD_SAVE_DIR` overrides the save directory (`src/save/paths.ts:9`). Write a save with `--out`, edit `savedAt` to a week earlier, and launch with `STEWARD_SAVE_DIR=<dir> bun run play`.

Every existing `prepareStartup` test must pass a fixed `wallNow`, or its fixture `savedAt` values (such as `2026-01-02` in `test/fixtures/save-and-die.ts:10`) would trigger catch-up.

### 7. droppedMs

`droppedMs` is time lost when a single frame exceeds 50 steps (5 s). That happens when the event loop stalls, and possibly when the machine sleeps with the game open. Bun's `performance.now()` on macOS may not advance during system sleep, so a laptop sleep may simply pause the game and never reach `droppedMs`. Folding it in would mean `Session` calling catch-up mid-session, outside `step`, and placing a return event during play. I would not fold it in for the first pass. Instead, keep the field and decide whether long stalls (say, over the grace window) should trigger a mid-session return, with the clock comment updated either way.

## Open questions

1. **Does the tick advance?** I recommend not: it freezes every absolute deadline and the season. The cost is that age-triggered audits never fire from an absence (share-triggered audits still do).
2. **Season while away.** Use the exit season's rate (leaving in winter yields no grain), or the yearly average yield for any absence longer than a year of game time (four minutes)?
3. **Births and the feed policy.** No births offline means grain fed to growth is wasted. The choices are to exclude feeding from the offline grain rate, to bank it into `growth` without admitting anyone, or to allow births up to `maxPopulation`.
4. **How hard the return debt hits.** Any meaningful offline rate makes a share-triggered audit arrive about 60 s after launch with 90 s to answer, and backing needs real stock for the whole debt. Is a near-certain failed audit after a long absence intended, or should the return grant a longer first warning?
5. **A full desk.** Should the return event push out the oldest entry (charging its expiry cost), be exempt from the slot limit like audits, or have an expiry of its own?
6. **What counts as extreme.** Which signals should scale the return cost: single-job labor, selling the whole harvest, a negative grain rate, or books "strained" or "rotten"? And should the cost scale with real time or compressed time?
7. **Governor voice.** Should the report be from a governor only when one serves (and inflated by their report factor unless true reports are owned), with a neutral clerk otherwise?

## Suggested PR breakdown

1. **Offline time and tuning (no dependencies).** Add a new `offline` tuning section with grace window, curve scale, offline fade rate and offline interest per real hour. Add a pure core function from real ticks to compressed ticks with no `Math.log`, and its unit tests.
2. **Catch-up in the core and at startup (depends on 1).** Add a core method that applies the projected rates for the compressed ticks (with the clamps and the special cases above), fades the conjured stock and charges interest on real ticks, without advancing the tick. Call it from `prepareStartup` with an injected `wallNow`, add a startup notice, and add the runner's `--away` flag.
3. **The return event (depends on 2).** Add the new desk kind placed without randomness, its templates and copy, a scale from time away and policy extremity (which needs `runFlows` to return `chosen`), and the optional governor-report phrasing. Add the exit-criterion test: a week away gives reduced gains, undiminished debt and a return event on the desk.
4. **Optional: droppedMs (depends on 2).** Either route long in-session stalls through catch-up or rewrite the clock comment to say the time is intentionally dropped.
